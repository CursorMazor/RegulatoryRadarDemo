TRAINING SUMMARY - verify against the official source.

The FDA has replaced the long-standing Quality System Regulation with the Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR). The new rule incorporates the international standard ISO 13485 by reference, so that the requirements for a medical device quality management system in the United States are largely the same as those already used in most other markets.

Some FDA-specific requirements remain in addition to the standard, for example around records, labeling and packaging controls, and the links between the quality system and FDA reporting obligations such as medical device reporting and corrections and removals. The way the FDA inspects manufacturers is also changing to reflect the new structure.

What this means for a company: organisations that are already certified to ISO 13485 should perform a gap assessment rather than a full rewrite, focusing on terminology (for example "management review" and "risk management" as the standard uses them), on the retained FDA-specific clauses, and on how procedures reference the regulation. Training records should show that staff understand the new terms. Companies not yet aligned with the standard have a larger project ahead and should plan it as a cross-functional programme rather than a documentation exercise.
