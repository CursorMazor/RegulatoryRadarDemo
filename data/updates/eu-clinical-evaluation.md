Clinical evaluation under the EU Medical Device Regulation is a continuous process rather than a one-time report. The manufacturer plans it, collects clinical data from literature, clinical investigations and post-market experience, appraises that data, and documents the conclusions in a clinical evaluation report that is updated throughout the life of the device.

The regulation raised the bar in several places. Claiming equivalence to another device is harder and needs access to that device's technical data. For implantable and class III devices, clinical investigations are generally expected unless specific exceptions apply, and a summary of safety and clinical performance must be made publicly available. Software that produces a clinical output, including AI-based software, needs clinical evidence that the output is valid and useful for the intended patients, not only evidence that the software works technically.

What this means for a company: review the clinical evaluation report for each product against the current expectations, check every equivalence claim, and plan post-market clinical follow-up so that the evidence base grows over time. Keep the literature search protocol current, since the report has to be refreshed regularly.
