TRAINING SUMMARY - verify against the official source.

The EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR) replaced the earlier directives, and devices that were certified under the old directives ("legacy devices") were given extra time to move over. The extended transition periods are not automatic: a manufacturer must meet conditions such as having a quality management system that complies with the MDR, having applied to a notified body for the new certificate by a set date, and not making significant changes to the design or intended purpose of the device in the meantime. The end dates differ by risk class, with higher-risk devices generally given less time.

Legacy devices on the market during the transition are still subject to MDR requirements for post-market surveillance, vigilance and registration, even though their technical documentation was assessed under the old rules.

What this means for a company: keep a register of every legacy certificate, its expiry date and the conditions attached to the transition. Coordinate with the notified body early because review capacity is limited. Treat any planned design change to a legacy device as a regulatory question first, since a significant change can end the transition for that device.
