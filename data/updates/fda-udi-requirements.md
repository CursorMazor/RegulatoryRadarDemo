TRAINING SUMMARY - verify against the official source.

The Unique Device Identification (UDI) system requires most medical devices distributed in the United States to carry a unique identifier on their label and packaging, both in plain text and in a form that machines can read, such as a barcode. The identifier has a device part that identifies the model and a production part that can carry the lot, serial number or expiration date. Manufacturers submit the device information to the FDA's Global Unique Device Identification Database (GUDID), where it is publicly searchable.

Some devices have additional expectations. Devices that are reusable and reprocessed between patients are expected to carry the identifier directly on the device itself. Implantable devices have specific considerations because the identifier supports patient records and recall management.

What this means for a company: every product needs a labeling check and a matching database record, and both must be updated when the labeling or the device changes. Because the EU has its own identification system and database, it is efficient to manage the two sets of requirements together so that identifiers, labels and records stay consistent across markets.
