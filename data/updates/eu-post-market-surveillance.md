TRAINING SUMMARY - verify against the official source.

Under the EU Medical Device Regulation, every manufacturer must run a post-market surveillance (PMS) system that actively collects and reviews experience with its devices once they are on the market. The system has to be planned in a PMS plan that is part of the technical documentation and has to be proportionate to the risk class and the type of device.

Higher-risk devices require a periodic safety update report (PSUR) that summarises the results of the surveillance, the conclusions of the benefit-risk assessment, sales volumes and an estimate of the population using the device. The PSUR is updated on a regular schedule and, for the highest classes, is reviewed by the notified body. Post-market data must also feed back into risk management, clinical evaluation and, where relevant, the instructions for use. Serious incidents and field safety corrective actions have their own reporting timelines under the vigilance rules.

What this means for a company: define the data sources for each product, such as complaints, service records, literature, registries and connected-device telemetry, and schedule the PSURs. Make sure PMS findings are visible to the teams that own risk management and clinical evaluation, not only to the complaint handling group.
